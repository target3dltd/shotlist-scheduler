    What this is

    A shoot scheduler and shot-naming tool for a whole production. Each project holds its details, cast and crew, settings and every shoot day. Change any setup or shoot time and the day's start and end times recalculate down the list. Mocap days add the motion-capture columns and build shot names with take numbers for the DIT.

    Four ways in: Clipboard, Slate, Details, Edit

    • The switcher at the top right moves between four views of the same data. Clipboard is the day's full shotlist, for planning and running the day. Slate is the full-screen slate (clap, reset, cut). Details is one shot at a time, with its storyboard, takes, notes and scribble (a sequence opens as Sequence View). Edit is the cutting room. The switcher is also inside each view, and moving between Slate and Details keeps the shot you're on. Esc, or ✕, goes back to the Clipboard.
    • On a phone the switcher has its own row under the header.

    How times are worked out

    • A day starts at Call / start. For each row: start = previous end + setup, end = start + shoot. Breaks and moves add their minutes.
    • Setup buffer % and Shoot buffer % scale every row at once (150% = everything takes half as long again). Breaks aren't scaled. The scaled minutes show under each box.
    • Min setup sets a floor for every setup before the buffer is applied.
    • Rows finishing after Target wrap turn red, and the wrap card shows how far over you are.

    Days

    • Each shoot day has a tab under the title with its own name, kind, day number, date, call, wrap, buffers and minimum setup. Once a day has timings, its tab shows the calculated wrap.
    • Kind: Live Action days (tagged LA) focus on setups and timings. Mocap days (tagged Mocap) also show Takes, Action, Performer(s), # Perf., Genre, Body / Face / Glove, Hero and Safety. Every column is available on both kinds: switch any on or off under Columns. Your choice is remembered separately for each kind.
    • The tabs are grouped. Ready to roll comes first, soonest date first, with undated days after the dated ones. Wrapped sits to the right, most recent first. The page opens on the next day that's ready to roll.
    • Day status (in the day banner; not the same as a shot's Status) decides the group. Auto treats a day as wrapped once its date has passed or every shot on it is ticked. Choose Ready to roll or Wrapped to set it by hand, for example to reopen a wrapped day for pickups. Status is shared with the team.
    • Ready to roll days fill the left two thirds of the tab bar and Wrapped days the right third. As days are added the tabs shrink to fit, shortening their names; hover over a tab for its full name, date and setup count. The day you're on always shows in full. When tabs are squeezed, Mocap days keep a slate line along the top of the tab.
    • Day # is used in shot names (for example d4).
    • + Day adds a day. Copy day duplicates the current day with the ticks and takes cleared, which is handy for planning a pickup day. Archive day puts the day away for everyone instead of deleting it: nothing is lost, and the Archived (n) tab at the end of the day tabs lists archived days with a Restore button. Archived days are left out of the CSV and .xlsx exports. Days can't be deleted, so a stray click never loses a day's work. Reset day puts an imported day back as it was imported. Deleting: the site admin (with the admin key saved in Settings > Projects) sees Delete next to each archived day, and Delete all. It asks first, says how many shots and takes go with it, and Undo straight after brings it back; after that it's gone for everyone, so export the takes first if you need them. A project kept only in this browser can always delete its own archived days. The site itself refuses to delete an archived day for anyone without the admin key.

    Editing

    • Click any text to edit it. Use − / + for minutes; the step size is a team default in Settings.
    • Drag the ⠿ handle to reorder, or use ↑ ↓. Dup copies a row, +Brk adds a break or move below, +Tech adds tech / turnaround time below, Del removes the row.
    • Jump to a time: click a row's Start time and type when you want it to happen, e.g. 14:30. Before you confirm it shows where the row will land; it then moves to the slot where it starts closest to that time, and everything after it re-times. Works for shots, breaks and tech rows, so it's a quick way to slot a new shot in or move lunch. One Undo puts it back.
    • Move on a shot sends it to another day, e.g. pushing shots to the next day. Pick the day (or a new one) and whether it goes at the start or the end. Tick more shots in the list to move them together, or click All not yet shot to push the rest of the day. The shot keeps its notes, pictures, priority and logged takes, its times are worked out for the new day, and its name picks up the new day number for the next takes. Copy leaves the original and adds fresh, unshot copies. One Undo puts it all back. The recovery plan offers Move… next to its cut suggestions too.
    • Tech / turnaround rows (purple) are for technical setups, resets, re-rigs and turnarounds: time the day needs that isn't a shot or a break. Add one with + Tech / turnaround under the table or +Tech on a row. Like breaks they just add their minutes (no buffer, no setup, no shot number, never suggested for cutting), but they're coloured differently, have their own total card and timeline colour, and show as Tech in the CSV and .xlsx. The Break / Tech tag on the row switches it between the two.
    • + Paste list of actions turns a pasted list (one action per line) into shots.
    • Tick Shot when a setup is done. Use Show → Not yet shot to hide finished and cut rows, or tick Hide cut shots (next to Show) to hide just the cut ones. Cut shots still keep their place; Undo or Cut again brings them back.
    • Click a reference image to enlarge it. Click an empty box to add an image, or hover and press ✕ to remove one.
    • Move columns: drag a column's heading left or right. A coloured line shows where it will land. A plain click still sorts. Your order is just for you and is used on every day. New columns slot in next to their usual neighbour. Columns ▾ → Reset column order puts them back.
    • Column widths: drag a column header's right edge. Double-click the edge to reset it.
    • Sort: click a header (▲ then ▼, a third click clears it). Sorting only changes the view; times still follow the running order. Make this the running order in the blue bar applies the sort to the schedule.
    • Filter: type in the box under a header. Start with ! to exclude (!insert). For numbers and times use > or <, e.g. Setup >10, End >17:30, Takes >2.
    • Undo / Redo: the buttons or Ctrl/Cmd+Z, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z. Undo only reverses your changes. A teammate's edits made in the meantime stay.

    Pace and captured time

    • Pace appears under the summary cards on a shoot day (and stays as the final score on a past day). The big number is how far ahead (−) or behind (+) the day is running, coloured green when you're 5 or more minutes up, blue on track, amber slipping and red behind.
    • Each finished shot gets a score: the minutes it won back (+, green) or lost (−, red) against the plan. Your streak counts the shots in a row that held or beat the plan, next to the day's best streak and the total won back and lost. The bar shows where the day will wrap against the target, from an hour early to an hour late.
    • It scores the time each shot was ticked as shot, so tick them as they finish (in the table, or Shot done in Slate View or Details). Turn the panel off in Settings > Display.
    • Takes and Captured (Mocap days) or Raw footage (Live Action days) cards appear once takes are logged: the day's takes, and the seconds recorded between each take's timecode in and out (Clap and Cut on the slate, or Roll and Cut in Details), with the average per take and the project total.
    • On Mocap days a Hero selects card sits next to Captured: the seconds in takes marked Hero (in Details), how many hero takes there are, and what share of the captured time they make up. Hero takes without a timecode out are counted but noted as not timed.

    Slate View and Details

    • In Slate View, tap the shot number to open that shot in Details.
    • Status (first column): To do, Setting up (you're on it, not rolling yet), ● Rolling, Shooting (takes logged, between takes), Shot ✓ or Cut. Click it to change: Setting up, ● Roll / ■ Cut take, Shot ✓, To do, or Cut shot. On the shoot day the next shot shows Setting up on its own until you roll; a sequence's shots show the sequence's status.
    • Stepping back: you can pick any status from the menu, forwards or backwards. To do puts a shot back to not started (if a take is rolling it's cut first, and the takes stay logged), and the shot stops showing Setting up even if it's next in the list, until you set it up or roll. Setting up on a shot that already has takes means a reset or re-rig: it shows Setting up until the next take rolls. From Shot ✓, either one unticks it. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z) also undoes the last change.
    • Which shot you're on: the live ahead / behind (the Running card, and the pill in Details) works from the shot or sequence you're on. Rolling a take puts you on it; when you shoot out of order, press We're on this in Details, Sequence View or the slate. Until a take rolls you're setting up: you go behind once its setup should have finished. Once rolling you're shooting: it times from the first take and you go behind once the shot should have finished. Ticking it done moves you on to the next. Hover the Running card to see how it's worked out.
    • Takes: ✕ removes a take and shows Undo straight away (Ctrl/Cmd+Z works too). Double-click the take number (T3) to renumber it, for example to match what's on the physical slate (the next take then follows on), or to give it its own clip name; leave the name empty to keep the automatic one. A renamed clip name goes into the takes export. Fix the timecode: double-click a take's TC (or its number) and change in or out, as HH:MM:SS:FF, if you rolled late or cut early; the take's length and the captured / raw seconds follow. Undo puts it back.
    • Arrange Details: drag a panel by its ⠿ (top right) to move it, or focus the ⠿ and use the arrow keys. Drag the corner at its bottom right to make it wider (in whole columns) and taller; double-click the corner to put that panel back to its usual size. Reset layout in the header puts everything back. The layout is kept on each device, so a laptop and a phone can be arranged differently. A playing video carries on while you rearrange.
    • Reloading the page keeps you where you were: Slate View or Details opens again on the same shot (in that browser tab). Close the view to go back to the table.
    • Slate (the red button at the end of the header) opens a full-screen slate for the next shot in the running order: the first shot on the open day not yet ticked as shot. ‹ › (or the arrow keys) step through the shots. It keeps the screen awake while open, and ⛶ makes it truly full screen where the browser allows.
    • Tap the striped bar (or Clap · log take, or the space bar) to clap: the whole slate flashes (inverts for at least 3 frames, and never under 120 ms) as the clap sound plays, giving the editor a picture sync point to line up with the sound. The flash is held back by the device's audio delay so the two land together. It shows even with Reduce motion on; turn it off in the slate settings (⚙) under Flash the screen on the clap. The take is logged on the shot with its timecode in, so the take number moves on. While it rolls the slate shows the take, its timecode in and how long it's been going. ■ Cut (or X) adds the timecode out and the length. Each take's in and out show in Details, and the .xlsx export fills its TC IN / TC OUT columns from them (tick the timing columns when exporting). Turn logging off in ⚙ if you only want the flash. It also plays a sound: a clap by default, or in ⚙ > Sound choose clapper sticks, a 1 kHz sync beep (handy for lining up sound), your own sound file (MP3, WAV or M4A, kept on this device), or none, and set the volume. On an iPhone the ring / silent switch has to be on ring to hear it. Shot done ✓ ticks the shot and moves to the next one.
    • ↺ Reset (or the E key) is for going again without cutting. While a take is rolling, each Reset marks R1, R2 and so on with its timecode. It's in Slate View (Clap / Reset / Cut), Sequence View and Details. The resets are listed under the take; click one to mark it Hero, Good, NG or Safety, or remove it. A hero reset fills the Hero column as, say, T3 R4. Resets are included in Export takes, and the Edit tool can cut a reset in at its own timecode (the T3·R4★ chips, or under Clip > Source). Use it only when you don't cut; for a normal new take, cut and clap again.
    • ⚙ Settings: choose what shows (production, director, camera / DOP, shot, take, roll, date, day, FPS, shot name, description, a custom line such as INT. TAVERN - NIGHT, timecode), the look (black, white, high contrast, classic), the roll / card (Next roll counts it on: A001, A002…) and the frame rate. Slate settings are kept on this device.
    • Timecode: Time of day uses this device's clock. Jam from LTC listens to the microphone input for linear timecode: plug a Tentacle Sync (or any LTC source) into the phone or tablet's mic input with a TRRS adaptor cable, set it to output audio timecode, and keep its level up. The slate locks on in a second or two, works out the frame rate (24, 25 or 30) from the signal, then free-runs from the device's clock like a hardware slate, so you can unplug. It's accurate to about a frame; jam again every few hours (the slate reminds you after 4). Or type a timecode and press Jam now. A jam is shared with the team (untick in ⚙ if you don't want that): every device on the project, e.g. the script supervisor's phone in Details, then logs takes on the same timecode for the next 12 hours, as long as its clock is set automatically. Time of day makes one device ignore it; Team jam goes back to it. If the project's frame rate is a capture rate (e.g. 120), the timecode runs at 25 unless you pick a rate.
    • Notes card in Details: Columns ▾ picks which columns you edit there (any of the shotlist's columns, your own included). Your choice is kept on this device; Back to the usual five resets it.
    • Edit card in Details: when the shot is in a cut, it shows how long the shot runs in each cut (seconds and frames), where it sits (record timecode), which take or reset it uses and how far in it starts, with Open in Edit. If the shot has a planned Screen dur., it says how far the cut is from it.
    • To open any shot in Details from the list, click the ↗ at the end of its Shot # (it appears as you hover over the row, and stays faint on a tablet or phone).
    • Details (the blue button at the end of the header) opens the current shot full screen: its storyboard pictures, description (click it to edit), script, location and shot name, plus:
      • Takes: ● Roll logs a take with its timecode in and ■ Cut adds the timecode out and length (if the take was clapped in Slate View, Cut finishes that same take); + Take (or T) is for a take you forgot to roll on: it adds the next take number with no timecode and nothing rolling; double-click its timecode to fill it in. ↺ Reset (or E) marks a go-again within a rolling take. Mark each take Good, NG, Hero or Safety and add a note. Hero and Safety fill the shot's Hero and Safety columns, so wrapped days ask for those takes' file links. Takes clapped in Slate View show here too.
      • Notes: director, camera, lighting, VFX and prop / set notes, saved straight onto the shot for the whole team.
      • Scribble: draw with a finger or pencil (pressure works on a tablet), in black, red or blue, with an eraser, undo and clear. Save to shot keeps it on the shot for everyone; Add to storyboard also puts it in the first empty Storyboard box.
      • Shot done ✓ ticks the shot and moves to the next; the header shows how far ahead or behind the day is running.
    • Both views work on phones, tablets and laptops in either orientation, and Escape closes them.
    • On a laptop:
      • Keys in Slate View: Space or C claps, X cuts, ← → change shot, F full screen. In Details: R roll / cut, T log a take, G N H S mark the last take Good / NG / Hero / Safety, ← →, F.
      • Audio input for LTC (in the slate's ⚙): laptops often have several inputs, so pick the one the Tentacle is plugged into: the headset socket (a TRRS cable), a USB-C headset adaptor or a USB audio interface. The built-in microphone won't pick up LTC reliably. The input names appear once the browser has been allowed to use the microphone.
      • Open the slate in its own window (in ⚙) to put it on a second monitor, projector or a screen facing camera while the schedule stays open on the laptop. Both windows stay in step.

    Edit / animatic

    • Edit in the header opens a cutting room for the project: a player, a timeline, and on the right the Shots list and the Clip settings. Use it to time an animatic from the boards, then build the cut as rushes or previz arrive.
    • Adding shots: pick a day in the Shots list. Click a shot's picture to add it after the selected clip, or drag it onto the timeline; + Add all adds the day's shots in order. Each shot comes in with its best picture: the hero take, then a good take, then its video link, then the storyboard frame. The chips let you choose: Board, a take (T3★ is a hero, T2✓ good) or Video.
    • Length starts from the shot's Screen dur. (or the take's length, or 3 seconds). Drag a clip's right edge to trim, or type the length in seconds under Clip, with −1f / +1f for single frames and Whole take. Drag clips to reorder; Split at playhead (S) cuts one in two.
    • Takes and video: a take uses the shot's video link (or a link you paste for that clip) as its picture and the take log's timecode for the EDL. The In point is how far into the take or video the clip starts; In at playhead sets it from where you've stopped. Video plays best from a direct file link (mp4 / mov, Dropbox, your own server). YouTube and Vimeo start at the in point. Google Drive previews can't be controlled from the page (press play inside them), unless the project has a Google API key for Drive (Settings > Project > Reference video): then Drive files shared as 'Anyone with the link' play straight in, on time and from their in point. Without a video the board is shown with the take number and timecode.
    • Swap in latest takes replaces boards (and older takes) with each shot's hero or good take, keeping your timings. A ★ on a clip means a better take is in.
    • Playing: Space plays and pauses, ← → step a frame (Shift: a second), ↑ ↓ jump between clips, Home / End. Add music or VO to time against: paste a link to an audio file (shared with the team), or From this device to play a file without uploading it. Clip sound turns on the sound from video clips.
    • Cuts: keep several (the ⋯ menu renames, duplicates or deletes). Cuts are shared with the team and saved as you go; if two people edit the same cut at once, the last change wins, so take turns.
    • Export EDL writes a CMX3600 EDL (Premiere, Resolve, Avid): takes come in with their clip name and source timecode, boards as AX events named after the shot, with the shot and your note as comments. Export FCP XML writes a Final Cut Pro 7 XML (Premiere, Resolve) with clip names, in and out points, the take timecode, and a marker per shot. Relink to the rushes in your edit software.
    • Import EDL / XML brings a cut back from the edit as a new cut. Clip names are matched to your shots and takes (DIT names, take clip names, _R resets, shot_board), and source timecode sets each take's in point. Anything that doesn't match comes in striped with its name; pick its shot under Clip > Shot.
    • Target (next to the running time) is the length you're cutting to, as minutes:seconds; the difference shows red when you're over.
    • Not in this cut (in the Shots list) shows only the shots you haven't used yet.
    • The clap: when you clap on the slate, the moment is kept on the take (or on its latest reset). The Edit tool starts that take half a second after the clap, the EDL notes the sync point, and the FCP XML puts a Clap marker on the clip.
    • Two people can work on the same cut at once: each clip saves on its own, so you don't overwrite each other.
    • On a phone, Import, Export and Swap in latest takes are in the cut's ⋯ menu.

    On the day: ahead / behind

    • Running card: on the day itself (the day's Shoot date is today) a card shows how far ahead (-, green) or behind (+) you are. It uses the time each shot was ticked as shot, against when the schedule had it finishing. If the shot you're on runs past its planned finish, the delta keeps growing until you tick it. Hover the card to see what it's based on. The card also shows the projected wrap, and a blue line marks now on the timeline. It updates every 30 seconds.
    • Breaks, lunch and tech have their own status chip on the row: Not yet, On now or Done ✓. When the last shot before lunch is done, the Running card shows Start Lunch; while it's on, it counts down ("20m left", or "5m over") with an End Lunch button. Ahead / behind then runs from when the break should end, setting up for the next shot starts when you end it, and the actual timeline draws the break at its real times. Starting a break cuts any take still rolling. Marking breaks is optional: if you don't, the planned break length is counted before the next setup, so the delta holds steady over lunch instead of drifting towards ahead.
    • Tick shots as they finish, so the delta is accurate. Shots ticked before this version, or on another day, have no time recorded, so the delta falls back to the clock.
    • Actual timeline sits under the planned timeline, on the same time scale, so you can see where the day went. For each shot or sequence it shows setting up (from when its Status was set to Setting up, or else from when the last shot finished) and shooting (from the first take to when it was marked Shot ✓). Breaks and tech rows between two shots are drawn at their planned length. A shot ticked with no takes logged shows striped, as setup and shooting can't be split. The one you're on pulses and grows with the clock. Its header totals setting up and shooting for the shots done so far against the plan. Hover a bar for its times, click it to jump to the shot.
    • Choosing the summary cards: tap the faint ⋯ at the end of the cards along the top (or Display settings > Summary cards) and untick any you don't need, such as Day length or Breaks. You can also turn off the hint pills under the cards (no lunch scheduled, shots with no setup time). Show all brings everything back. Per device, so the DIT and the 1st AD can each have their own.
    • Layouts: in the ⋯ menu at the end of the cards (or Display settings > Layouts), pick 1st AD, DIT / data, Director or Everything to set the cards, panels and focus in one go; your columns stay as they are. Type a name and Save to keep your own layout, which also remembers your columns and their order. A * on a layout means you've changed things since picking it. Layouts are per device.
    • Shoot-day focus: the Focus button next to the cards (shown on the shoot day, or whenever focus is on) tucks away what you only need for planning: the Day length, Setup / Shooting / Breaks / Tech totals and Original wrap cards, the hint pills, the planned timeline, and the Orig. start, Screen dur. and Hero / Safety link columns. Click it again to bring everything back. Nothing is deleted or changed for anyone else.
    • Pace in the Running card: the Running card shows your streak and the minutes won back and lost. Tap it to open the full pace panel underneath, tap again to close it. Prefer it separate? Turn off Display settings > Pace inside the Running card.
    • Recovery plan stays as a single line while you're on target or only slightly over (click it to open). At the amber or red gate it opens by itself.
    • Documents (in the header) has the Call sheet, Running order, Wrap report, DIT names, Export takes and Shoot-ready check together. On a phone they're in the ☰ Menu.
    • Phone mini-bar: on a phone, once you scroll past the cards a thin bar pins to the top with the delta, the shot you're on and the projected wrap (before the day: the day name, shots to go and the wrap). Tap it to go back to the top.
    • Showing and folding panels: Pace, Recovery plan, Planned timeline and Actual timeline can each be turned off in Display settings > Table and on-set tools. Each has a header bar: click it to fold the panel away to one line (it still shows the headline, such as the delta) and click again to open it. These settings are per device.
    • Recovery plan (under the summary cards) appears when the projected wrap is past target, or there are wins or cut shots. Its colour shows the gate: green on target, grey slightly over, amber and red past the gates you set in Settings > Team defaults (10 and 30 minutes over target wrap to start with). Before the day, it works from the planned wrap.
    • Shots to consider cutting are ranked by the minutes they take (setup plus shooting, with buffers) against their priority and screen duration: low priority, long setups and short screen time go first. The list stops once it covers the time you need. Top priority shots only appear as a last resort. The shot you're on is never suggested.
    • Cut takes a shot out of the timings without deleting it: the row greys out, it's left off the DIT names, the tracker .xlsx and print, and the CSV marks it Cut. Restore (in the row or the plan) puts it back, and the plan says when a cut shot would fit in the time you have in hand. Undo works too.
    • Possible wins spot a shot that goes back to a location you've already set up earlier. Move it puts it straight after that shot and drops its setup to the day's minimum.

    Hero / safety links

    • When a day is Wrapped, a Hero / safety links panel lists every shot marked hero or safety and asks for a link to its file, for example a Google Drive link. A shot counts when its Hero or Safety column is filled in, or its notes say e.g. "Hero: T3". Cut shots are left out.
    • A reminder shows under the summary cards, and when you open the day, until every link is in. Paste a link and the ↗ opens it. Anything that doesn't look like a web link is outlined in red.
    • Links are shared with the team. They also live in the Hero link and Safety link columns (shown on Mocap days, hidden on Live Action days until you switch them on in Columns), and go into the CSV and .xlsx exports.

    Columns and priority

    • Column group colours: related columns share a subtle tint so the table is easier to scan: Shot (number, priority, name), Timing, Capture (takes, performers, hero and safety), Location, Story (description, script), Camera & look (shot type, camera, lighting), Notes and Storyboard. Change the strength or turn it off in Settings > Display. Give your own columns a group in Settings > Columns. Print keeps the header tints only.
    • Camera and Lighting are separate columns. Older notes that mixed both stay in Camera, so move any lighting notes across if you want them split.
    • Settings → Columns adds your own columns. They are shared with the team and appear on every day. Each one can be text, long text, a number, a tick box or a dropdown (list the options with commas). Text cells wrap to show everything you type; Enter moves on rather than adding a line (long text keeps its line breaks). Reorder them with ↑ ↓ and delete with ✕. Deleting keeps the values behind the scenes, so Undo brings the column back with its data.
    • Tick In shot name to add a column to the generated names, then set its prefix and position in Settings → Shot names. A tick-box column adds its prefix (or its name) when ticked.
    • Rename built-in columns for the whole team, e.g. Hero to Hero code. Clear the box to go back to the original name.
    • Priority is on every day, colour-coded in its dropdown and down the row's left edge. Sorting by Priority puts the highest first. In Settings → Columns you can change the options and pick a colour for each one (shared with the team). The default is the highlight colour for the first option, then amber and grey. Text on the colour turns dark or light automatically so it stays readable.
    • The Priority dropdown next to Show filters the open day to one priority, a priority and higher (e.g. MID and higher), or rows with no priority set. It's just for you. Breaks are hidden while it's on, and times still follow the full running order. The blue bar shows the filter and clears it.
    • The Columns menu in the toolbar shows or hides columns just for you, separately for Mocap and Live Action days. Hide all not in use hides every column that is empty on all days of that kind (the number shows how many); Show all brings every column back.

    Shot names and DIT

    • The Shot name column shows the name the next take will get. Copy puts it on the clipboard.
    • DIT names lists every shot on the day. Next take → copy logs the take and copies its name, e.g. 012_T4_1001_d4_a1_Alex_walk_cycle_yB. Use − if a take didn't happen. Copy all copies every take name for a shot, one per line. Copy every take name does the whole day.
    • Settings → Shot names decides what goes into the name and in what order, with prefixes and tags, the separator, what happens to spaces, and how many digits shot and take numbers get. These settings are shared with the whole team, so everyone generates the same names.
    • Blank parts and - are skipped, so there are never double separators. Characters that break file names (/ \ : * ? " < > |, commas and brackets) are removed. A slash between names becomes +, e.g. Alex+Sam.

    Projects, cast and crew

    • Settings → Project holds the project name, client, production ID, frame rate, dates, location, capture system, lead and notes. Click the project name in the title bar to jump there.
    • Settings → Cast & crew keeps names, roles, phone, email and notes. To reorder, drag the ⠿ handle at the start of a row, or use the ↑ ↓ buttons; the order is shared with the team. You can export it as a CSV.
    • Settings → Projects: open, create (blank, same settings without shots, or a full copy), remove, export a project file, or import one. Shotlist Builder project files (.json) and older Scheduler saves import too. A Builder file becomes a Mocap day, with retakes counted as takes.

    Spreadsheets

    • File ▾ > Import (or drop a file on the page) reads .xlsx or .csv: exports from Google Sheets, Excel or Numbers, the Shotlist Builder, or your Shotlist tracker. Pick the sheet and header row, then choose where each column goes. Columns with familiar names (Action, Performer(s), Prop, Take #, Shoot Day, Notes and so on) are matched for you.
    • Storyboard pictures from a PDF (File ▾ > Import, pick the PDF): in Keep them mode, a picture the shot already has (the same frame from a spreadsheet, at any size) isn't added again. File ▾ > Remove duplicate pictures tidies shots that got the same frame twice, and one Undo puts them back.
    • A revised shot list from the client: import it the same way. When most of its shot numbers are already in the project, the import picks the shots already in this project (update them, matched by Shot #). Check the column mapping, then Import shows every change before anything happens: which shot, which column, what it was and what it will be, how many shots are newly cut and any new shots. Apply makes them in one go; one Undo takes the lot back.
      • Shot numbers match however they're written: 005, 5, 100.0 and sh030 find 05, 100 and 030.
      • Only days that look like the same shot list are updated, so another sequence that also has a shot 5 isn't touched.
      • Kept as they are: setup and shoot times, the running order, takes, ticks, columns the sheet doesn't have, and storyboard pictures already in place (pictures only fill shots that have none).
      • A Status column (or one headed Hero shot) cuts shots marked CUT and puts back ones that no longer are; any other word in it, such as HERO SHOT, becomes the shot's priority and is added to the priority list.
      • New shots go after the shot before them in the sheet. Shots in the project that the sheet doesn't have are listed and left alone.
    • Group retakes merges consecutive rows with the same Shot # and Action into one shot with a take count. Hero / Safety / NG marks per take are noted in Director notes, e.g. Hero: T7.
    • Tick Rows are takes already recorded when importing a log of what was shot. Leave it off for a plan, so the first take you log is T1.
    • Headings that don't match anything are set to + New column, which creates a project column named after the heading. Its type is worked out from the values: tick box, number, dropdown or text. Change any of them to Ignore if you don't want them.
    • Send the shots to a new day, to new days split by the Shoot Day column, to the open day, or to a new project. A Builder export's Project tab fills in the project details. Undo takes an import back.
    • Exports include your own columns and any renamed headings.
    • Export .xlsx writes the Shotlist Builder tracker for Google Sheets or Excel: a Project tab with cast and crew, then a Shotlist with one row per take. Take # and Shot ID are live formulas driven by an ID Settings tab, so retakes added in the sheet number themselves. Optional timecode and frame columns and a Schedule tab are included. In Google Sheets: File > Import > Upload > Replace spreadsheet.
    • Round trip: the exported .xlsx keeps the tracker's own column names (Prop, Technical Notes = Camera, Lighting = Lighting, Capture Notes = VFX notes, Notes = Director notes) so it lines up with your Google Sheets tracker. It also carries a hidden App data tab and a hidden App row ID column. When you import it back, choose the days as exported (picked for you): each day returns with its name, kind (Mocap or Live Action), date, times and buffers, and every field, including the ones the tracker has no column for (script, screen duration, original start, storyboard refs, your own columns with their types). Anything you edited in the Shotlist tab wins, rows you deleted there stay deleted, and rows you added are put on the day matching their Shoot Day. Leave the hidden tab and column in place for this to work.
    • On the team site the spreadsheet library is served from the site itself. The standalone file fetches it from a public library server the first time you import or export.

    Settings

    • Team defaults (shared): − / + step, setup and shoot minutes for new shots, the break name and length, the tech / turnaround name and length, and the call, wrap, buffers and minimum setup for new days.
    • Display (just you, on this device). If your eyes get tired, start with a preset: Easy on the eyes (warm paper colours, Atkinson Hyperlegible, bigger text, more space, striped rows), Night / dark set, High contrast or Compact. Then fine-tune:
      • Colour theme: match device, light, warm paper (less glare), dim (soft grey dark), dark, high contrast and high-contrast dark. The ◐ button flips between your chosen light and dark themes.
      • Highlight colour for selections, warnings and the wrap line.
      • Font: System, Atkinson Hyperlegible (designed for low-vision readability), Lexend (wider, calmer letterforms), serif, rounded or monospace. The team site serves the fonts itself.
      • Text weight, interface and table text size, line spacing, letter spacing and row spacing (compact, comfortable, roomy).
      • Stripe alternate rows to help follow a row across a wide table, reduce motion to stop the highlight flashes, and a 12 or 24-hour clock.
      • Show − / + buttons on Setup and Shoot times: untick to hide the steppers and just type the minutes.
      • Highlight rows the team are working in (off by default): on the team site, a row a teammate is in gets an outline and their initials, in the same colour as their badge in the header. Rows they change flash in that colour too. With it off you still get a note when someone updates the schedule.
      • Timeline and banner colours: pick your own colours for setup, shooting, break / move, tech / turnaround and the target wrap line on the timeline (the Timeline colours link under the timeline jumps here), and the colour and strength of the day banner behind the day settings and filters. Theme default goes back to your theme's colour.

    Offline and rejoining

    • Work offline instead on the join screen opens a separate copy saved only in this browser; it doesn't sync. Your team schedule is untouched.
    • To get back, click Rejoin team project next to the status in the header, or in Settings > Team & sync. Adding ?team to the end of the site address does the same.
    • If you lose signal on set without choosing offline, you don't need to do anything: changes queue on the device and send when the connection is back.

    Storyboard pictures

    • Each shot holds up to 3 pictures in the Storyboard column. Click an empty box to add one, click a picture to see it large, ✕ to remove it. A third box appears once the second is filled.
    • From a PDF: Import (or drop the file on the page) takes a shot tracker or storyboard PDF, such as a Google Sheets export. It needs a column headed Shot No (or Shot #) with the pictures on the same rows. Rows with a picture but no shot number are extra frames for the shot above, even across a page break. A wide sheet printed as several pages with the same rows is only read once.
    • You see every shot with its pictures before anything changes. Choose whether to match shots on every day or just this one, whether to keep pictures a shot already has (filling empty boxes) or replace them, and whether to add shots that aren't in the project yet. Shot numbers match ignoring leading zeros (005 = 5). One Undo takes the whole import back.
    • Keeping pictures when you export: the .xlsx export puts each shot's pictures in Storyboard 1-3 columns on its first take row, and the project file (Settings > Projects > Export project file) packs them in too. Importing either one, into this project, another project or another site, brings the pictures with it. Pictures placed on the rows of a sheet from elsewhere (Excel, Google Sheets) come in the same way.
    • Pictures belong to their project: with project keys on, only people holding that project's key can see them.

    Sequences: shots shot in one go

    • Group: click Seq in a row's buttons (or the + in the Sequence column), name it if you like, tick the shots and Group. Each shot keeps its own row, storyboard and notes; the rows get a coloured edge and a chip with the sequence's name. Click the chip, or Seq again, to change or ungroup it.
    • One set of takes: the sequence is slated, rolled and cut as one. Its DIT name uses the shot range: 162-166 when the shots sit together in the list, or 162+164+166 when they don't. The takes are kept on the sequence's first shot, so the take count, captured seconds, wrap report and takes export include them once.
    • Sequence View (click the chip, the banner in Details, or tap the shot number in Slate View): the shots side by side with their storyboards, the DIT name to copy, ● Roll / ■ Cut, + Take, Good / NG / Hero / Safety and a note per take, sequence notes, and Sequence done (ticks every shot). ‹ › moves between the day's sequences; the same keys work as in Details.
    • Live Action: every storyboard shot still needs catching, so each take has a tick per shot (or All). The shots show which takes caught them and Still to get lists what's missing. NG takes don't count. Mocap: a take records the whole action, so there are no ticks; framing is dealt with in post.
    • Slate View shows the sequence (and its range) for any of its shots, and Clap logs the sequence's take.
    • In the table each sequence has a header row: its name, range, start and end, takes and (Live Action) how many shots are caught. ▾ folds the sequence into that row; fold all / unfold all appear on the first one. Folding is just for your screen.
    • Rename: double-click the sequence's name (in its header row, the Sequence column or Sequence View), type, Enter. One shot at a time: the ▾ next to a shot's sequence moves it to another sequence, takes it out, or starts a new sequence with it; the + on a shot that isn't in one adds it to a sequence. If you take out a sequence's first shot, the sequence and its takes carry on with the next.
    • Moving things around a sequence: drag a break, lunch or shot onto a sequence's header row, folded or not: drop on its top half to put it above the sequence, its bottom half for below. Drag the header's ⠿ to move the whole sequence (all its shots together). If a break, lunch or another shot ends up between a sequence's shots, the header row says so: move after or move before puts it outside the sequence in one click.
    • Start a sequence at a time: click its times on the header row (e.g. 09:00 → 10:15), type the time it should start and press Enter. All its shots move together to the slot that starts closest; the preview says where before you confirm.
    • Reference video for a sequence: Sequence View has its own video panel. Paste Google Drive (or other) links there, as in Details; it sits beside the shots once there's a video, and keeps playing while you mark takes. Shots that have their own videos show ▶ shot video.
    • Time as one (header row or Sequence View): one setup and one shoot time for the whole sequence instead of per shot. It starts from the shots' longest setup and their shoots added up; type over them. The shots then show in seq. Time per shot switches back, with each shot's own times as they were.
    • Duplicate (header row or Sequence View): a copy of the sequence straight after it, with the same shots, storyboards and timings, no takes, named "(2)", "(3)"... Handy for another pass. The copies stay linked: when you change the original or a copy (its timing, notes, or which shots are in it), you're asked whether to make the same change to the others. Shots added are copied in; a shot taken out goes from the copies too (unless a copy has already shot it, when it just leaves that sequence).
    • Editing a sequence's shots in Sequence View: Edit shots lists the sequence's own shots first, ticked, then the rest of the day. Under each shot, Move… puts it in another sequence (or starts a new one) and Remove takes it out of the sequence; it stays on the day.
    • From the sheet: a column headed Seq or Sequence groups shots with the same letter or name (/, -, n/a and none mean not in a sequence). When you update from a revised sheet, the preview lists each sequence's shots and offers to put the running order in sequence order (A, B, C... with shot numbers in order inside each, then the shots not in a sequence; breaks and tech rows stay where they are). One Undo takes it back.
    • VCS: tick the VCS column (in Camera & look) for shots where the virtual camera is recorded into the take. It shows on the slate, in Details and in Sequence View. Shown by default on Mocap days; on Live Action days turn it on from the Columns menu.

    Reference and rehearsal video

    • Each shot can have one or more video links. In Details paste a link into the box beside the player (or type links into the Reference video column in the table, separated by spaces). The video plays in Details's top row, beside the storyboard, and keeps playing while you mark takes. A shot without a video has a small Add video box at the end.
    • Google Drive: right-click the video, Share, then Copy link. It plays in Drive's own player (with its speed, quality and full screen controls) for anyone who can open the file: share it with the team, or set it to Anyone with the link. A Drive folder link shows the folder; link each shot to its own file to play it in place.
    • Dropbox, Vimeo, YouTube and direct .mp4 / .mov / .webm links work too. Direct files (including Dropbox) get extra controls: quarter, half or full speed, one frame back or forward, loop and mirror (handy for blocking). Other links open in a new tab.
    • The videos stay where they are (your Drive); the app only keeps the links, so file size doesn't matter. Playing needs an internet connection on set.
    • The ▶ in the Reference video column opens that shot in Details.
    • Per project (Settings → Project → Reference video): choose where this project's videos live. For Google Drive, each person can enter their own Google account for the project (the one that can see its files): Drive then opens the videos with it even if they're signed into several. It's kept on their device, so nobody overwrites anyone else's, and Details shows which account a video is playing as. Add the project video folder link for a quick way to browse. For your own server or NAS, give the folder's web address (https://…) and the team can type just a file name, such as SH030_rehearsal.mp4. OneDrive / SharePoint: use the video's Embed option and paste the embed code. Box shared links play in Box's player.
    • Browsers can't play from FTP. Most NAS and FTP hosts can also share files over HTTPS, which is what's needed.

    Shoot-day tools

    • Shoot-ready check (Documents menu): a checklist for the night before. It checks the day is set up and the site is connected, shows who has joined, and has buttons to download a project backup, play the clap, open Slate View, practise a 30 second dropout (the status goes Offline, your changes wait, then send themselves), send a test feedback report and freeze timings. Ticks are kept on your device.
    • Freeze timings (day banner): once you're rolling, locks the day's setup and shoot minutes, the running order, call time and buffers, so nobody retimes or drags shots by accident. Ticking shots, takes, notes, text, marks and Cut still work for everyone. Unfreeze any time. A frozen day says so above the table.
    • Call sheet (Documents menu): one page with crew call, first shot, lunch and wrap, the location with weather and daylight, notes, cast and crew with phone numbers, and the running order. Type the address and notes straight into it; they're saved with the day and shared. Print / PDF prints just the call sheet (portrait), Copy as text is ready to paste into an email or message.
    • Wrap report: plan against actual. From the actual timeline: each shot or sequence's real setting up and shooting time against the plan, breaks at their real length, the totals, and the setups that ran longest over. Hero, good and safety resets are listed under Marked (e.g. T3 R4 hero).
    • DIT names lists marked resets under each shot as chips (T3 R4 ★): click one to copy its name, the take's name with _R4 on the end.
    • Call sheet: emergency and standard sections. The red Emergency box has the emergency number (999; change it for shoots abroad), the nearest A&E, police station and fire station, first aid (first aider or medic, where the kit is) and the fire assembly point. Find nearest A&E, police, fire looks them up from the day's location on OpenStreetMap (no location yet? it uses a postcode from the address, or opens Set location; if one server is busy it tries others, and says what happened next to the button): always check them, especially that the hospital has a 24-hour A&E. Copy from brings over another day's details (it picks a day at the same location if there is one). There's also Call times (one line per department), Travel and parking, and at the end the standard sections: Health and safety, Confidentiality, Code of conduct and Green note come pre-written; edit them once and every call sheet in the project uses your wording (Back to the standard wording undoes it). Add your Invoicing details and a Company line for under the title. Untick include to leave a section off. Anything left empty isn't printed. Everything here is shared with the team.
    • Call sheet: kit and risk assessment. Kit, suppliers and deliveries is a table for the day: supplier or department, what kit, contact, and delivery / collection times (+ Add supplier; Copy from another day when it's empty). Risk assessment starts with eight common film-set risks (slips and trips, electrical, falling stands, hot lights, manual handling, working at height, fire, welfare) with who may be harmed, the risk before and after, and the controls. Edit, add or remove rows to fit the shoot; it's shared by every call sheet in the project (Back to the starter risks resets it). Add a link to the full risk assessment underneath. Untick include to leave either off; empty rows aren't printed.
    • Wrap report (Documents menu): shots done out of planned, call and actual wrap against the target, takes, captured or raw seconds, hero selects, each shot's takes, marks and finish time against the plan, what carries over (with a button to move it to another day), cuts and your wrap notes.
    • Running order (Documents menu): one page for the day with times, each shot or sequence (one line per sequence, listing its shots), location and minutes, plus breaks and tech. Untick One line per sequence to list every shot. Print / PDF, Copy as text, or Download CSV.
    • Export takes (Documents menu, and in the wrap report for one day): a CSV with one row per take: clip name, take, mark, timecode in and out, duration, when it was logged, take note, shot details and hero / safety links. Opens in Excel or Sheets, or hand it to the edit or DIT.
    • Weather and daylight: give a day a location (Set location under the day banner, or in the call sheet) by town, UK postcode, latitude and longitude, or Use where I am now. You then see sunrise, golden hour and sunset (worked out on your device), plus the forecast once the day is within 16 days: conditions, high and low, the chance of rain and when it's likeliest, and wind in mph. Only the place name or position is sent, to the free Open-Meteo forecast service (and postcodes.io for postcodes). Mocap days only show it when you set a location.

    Who's online

    • The coloured letters next to Live are the people with this project open, with a count beside them. A faded letter means that person has been away for a few minutes.
    • Click them for the full list: each person's name, the day and shot they're working in, whether they're on a phone, tablet or computer, whether they're in Slate View or Details, and how long they've been on. Go there jumps to their day, scrolls to their row and outlines it in their colour (clearing your filters if they hid it). Someone in Slate View or Details is shown on the shot they have open.
    • Earlier today lists people who had the project open in the last 12 hours, with when they were last seen.
    • Change your name: click the letters, then Change name under your own entry (or Settings → Team & sync). Only shown on the team site.

    Feedback: bugs, ideas and questions

    • Back to the top: once you scroll down the list, a round ↑ button appears in the bottom right. Tap it to jump back to the top (handy on a phone with a long shotlist).
    • The ? button in the bottom left opens a short form. Pick Bug, Feature idea or Question, add a title and the details, and send. Include technical details (on by default) adds the version, browser, screen size, project and day, which makes bugs much easier to track down; you can see exactly what's sent before sending.
    • Reports are kept on your own site. Whoever holds the admin key sees an Inbox tab in the same panel, with a red badge on the ? for new ones, and can mark each New, Planned or Done, or delete it. Without an admin key set, everyone on the team site can see the inbox.
    • The admin also gets a pop-up note when a new one arrives while the app is open, and can allow alerts on that computer from the inbox.
    • Alerts when the app is closed (site owner, set up once in Netlify):
      • Phone push: install the free ntfy app, subscribe to a long, hard-to-guess topic name, then add an environment variable FEEDBACK_WEBHOOK = https://ntfy.sh/your-topic-name in Netlify (Site configuration → Environment variables) and redeploy. Slack, Discord and Google Chat incoming-webhook URLs work too.
      • Email: in Netlify open Forms, enable form detection, redeploy, then under Form notifications add an email notification for the feedback form.

    Tablets and phones

    • The tool works on iPads and other tablets, and on phones for quick checks and ticking shots off. Add it to your home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen) and it opens like an app.
    • On narrower screens the Shot # column stays put while you swipe sideways along a row, so you always know which shot you're in.
    • The day banner folds down to the Show and Priority filters. Tap Day settings to open the name, status, times and buffers.
    • Touch screens (iPad, phones): the shot list scrolls with the page, so one swipe carries straight through. Swipe sideways on the table to see more columns; Status and Shot # stay pinned on the left. On a computer the list keeps its own scroll area so the column headings stay in view.
    • On a phone the header is one row: undo, redo, ☰ Menu, Slate and Notes. The menu has Settings, Help, Share, Columns, the documents (Call sheet, Running order, Wrap report, DIT names, Export takes, Shoot-ready check) and light / dark, as well as Import, Export and Print.
    • Day tabs, the summary cards and the Settings tabs scroll sideways.
    • Buttons and the − / + steppers get bigger on touch screens. Drag a column's right edge with your finger to resize it. To move a row, use the ↑ ↓ buttons in the row's last column if dragging the ⠿ handle is fiddly.

    Working as a team

    • Share (in the header) gives you a link that opens this project, plus a ready-written invite you can copy, email or send with your phone's share sheet. Tick Also add them to Cast & crew to put them on the crew list too.
    • Passwords: the safest way is to send the link and the site password separately, e.g. the link by email and the password by text or in person. The invite leaves them out unless you tick to include them. If you include them, send it only to that person. The password you type there isn't saved anywhere. To lock someone out later, change the Netlify site password (Site configuration > Access control) and send the new one to the people still on the production.
    • On the team site, everyone with the team key edits the same live schedule. The coloured initials show who's online. The dot next to them shows the connection: Live, Saving… or Offline. Teammates' changes appear within a few seconds. Turn on Highlight rows the team are working in (Settings > Display) to see which row each person is in, and their changes flash, in their badge colour.
    • No lost work when two people edit at once. Every change is saved as its own small edit (this field on this row) and never overwrites the whole schedule. Different fields, even on the same row, simply combine. If two people change the same field at the same moment, the later save is what everyone sees, and both changes are listed in Activity.
    • Moving rows uses positions, not row numbers, so reordering while someone else adds or edits rows merges cleanly.
    • Deleting keeps the data behind the scenes, so Undo, or a teammate's later edit, never resurrects a half-deleted row.
    • Offline: changes are kept on your device and sent when the connection returns, even after closing the browser. The status shows how many are waiting.
    • Activity lists recent changes with who made them and when.
    • Settings → Team & sync: your display name, how often to check for changes, Sync now, and Forget team key.

    Saving and backups

    • Daily backups (team site): before the first change of each day the site keeps the whole project as it stood, for 30 days. Settings > Projects > Daily backups lists them: Download one as a project file, or (the admin, or anyone on a site without an admin key) Restore it, which puts the whole project back to that point for everyone. Undo straight after a restore puts it all back.
    • On your own, everything saves in this browser automatically. For a backup, or to move a project, use Settings → Projects → Export project file.
    • Export CSV gives one row per shot for every day, with Day, Kind and Date columns, calculated times, takes and the last take's name. It opens in Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets, and most shotlist apps can import it.
    • Print / PDF prints the open day in landscape A4, using the columns that are showing. Only the tab being printed is shown. Rows stay whole on a page, so every page ends on a cell line; only a row taller than a whole page is split. Text prints small (about 6.5pt, tight line spacing) whatever your on-screen text size, and columns are squeezed to fit the page width, with the notes columns giving way first; your on-screen widths aren't changed. Hide columns you don't need on paper with the Columns menu first for roomier notes. Dark mode prints light.

    Updates

    • Updating the site never touches the schedule: the shared data lives in the site's storage, not in the code that gets replaced.
    • If a new version goes live while you have the page open, a bar offers Refresh now. It sends any unsaved changes first. Changes that can't be sent yet (offline) stay on your device and go after the refresh.
    • Test versions (deploy previews and branch deploys) use separate storage, so trying something out can't change the live schedule.

    Netlify setup

    • The team version needs the site deployed from GitHub (or the Netlify CLI), not drag-and-drop, because only those deploys run the small server part that stores the shared schedule (Netlify Functions and Netlify Blobs).
    • Access is controlled by the Netlify site password, which covers the page and the data. If you ever want a second lock, set the environment variable TEAM_KEY and everyone will also be asked for that key. Without either, anyone with the link could open the schedule.
    • Data lives in the site's Netlify Blobs store. Nothing is sent to any other service. No project data is kept in the code or the GitHub repo, so a new site always starts empty.

    Separate teams and private projects

    • Project keys keep jobs for different teams or clients private from each other inside one site, so you can still jump between them. They're off until you switch them on.
    • Switching them on (once): in Netlify, Site configuration > Environment variables, add ADMIN_KEY with a long random value (20 or more characters; a password manager can make one), then redeploy. Then open the site, go to Settings > Team & sync and enter the admin key, or paste it into the Project key box when you join. Keep the admin key to yourself: it opens every project.
    • Give each project a key: Settings > Projects lists every project with a Create key button. Until a project has a key, only the admin can open it, so give one to each project as soon as keys are on and send it to that project's team. New projects get a key straight away.
    • What each person sees: only the projects whose keys they've entered. Everything for a project (schedule, edits, activity, storyboard images) is only handed over by the server to someone holding its key or the admin key. People without the key don't see its name in the project list.
    • Joining: people enter the key once per device, when they join or from Settings > Projects > Add project. Share includes the key, or reminds you to send it separately.
    • When someone leaves a job: Settings > Projects > Change on that project. The old key stops working at once, and devices still using it drop the project and its copy straight away. Send the new key to the people staying on. Other projects aren't affected.
    • Remove from this device takes a project and its key off a laptop or tablet, e.g. a shared machine.
    • Everyone still needs the Netlify site password to reach the site at all; the project keys decide what they can open once they're in. Anyone with access to your Netlify account can see all the data.
    • Separate sites are the alternative for a client who needs complete separation: in Netlify, Add new site > Import an existing project > GitHub, pick the same repository, give it its own name and its own site password. It has its own data, starts empty, and gets every update automatically.
    • To move a project between sites: Settings > Projects > Export project file, then Import on the other site.
